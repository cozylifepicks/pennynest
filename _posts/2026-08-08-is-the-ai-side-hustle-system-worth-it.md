## 11 things to know before you buy it

### 1. It sells leverage, not a shortcut

The core argument is the one honest thing about the AI hustle boom: most people
quit content because it eats 20–30 hours a week, not because they lack talent.
Handing the drafting to a tool and keeping the direction for yourself is a real
change in the maths. It is still an hour a day, every day. Nothing here removes
the hour.

### 2. The prompt library is the part you'd struggle to build yourself

Anyone can ask ChatGPT for a blog post. Very few people can write a hundred
prompts that reliably produce a *usable* one, because that takes months of
noticing which phrasings fail. A tested prompt library is the piece with genuine
time value, and it is the reason a $27-ish guide can save a $0 approach.

### 3. It assumes zero experience — which cuts both ways

If you've never opened ChatGPT, the beginner framing is exactly right. If you
already publish regularly and just want a sharper workflow, a chunk of the guide
will be ground you've covered, and you'll be buying it for the prompts and the
calendar alone. Be honest about which of those two people you are.

### 4. Check the refund window before you pay, not after

At the time of writing the checkout page states a **60-day money-back
guarantee**, and Digistore24 handles the refund rather than the seller. That is
unusually long for a digital product, and it is the single thing that makes
trying it low-risk. Read the terms on the checkout page yourself — policies do
change, and a promise on a sales page is not the same as the one you agree to.

### 5. Delivery is instant, but it is a download

You get the material immediately after payment. That trips up people expecting a
membership site with a dashboard and a community. There isn't one. If accountability
is the thing you personally need, this is not the format that gives it to you.

### 6. "1 hour a day" only works if the hour is real

The routine is genuinely designed around one hour. The failure mode is not the
plan; it is scheduling the hour somewhere it never survives — after the kids are
down, at the end of a long shift, on a Sunday night. Put it where your energy
actually is, even if that means 20 minutes before work three times a week
instead.

### 7. Income is a maybe, and anyone who says otherwise is selling

Content businesses pay slowly. Blogs and channels take months to earn anything,
and most never earn much. Buy this as a way to *start publishing consistently*,
which is the only precondition for the rest. If you need money this month,
[a same-week side hustle](/side-hustles-you-can-start-this-week/) that trades
hours for cash is the better call, and it costs nothing to begin.

### 8. It won't fix a spending problem

Extra income leaks out of a household exactly as fast as ordinary income does.
If nothing is left over now, nothing will be left over then either. Get the
[payday routine](/payday-routine/) working first — it takes an evening — and the
side income has somewhere to land.

### 9. Multi-platform is a strength and a trap

Blogs, YouTube, social and email from one system sounds efficient, and the
adaptation techniques are useful. But starting on four platforms is the most
reliable way to quit all four. Pick one, run the 90 days, then expand. The guide
says as much; people ignore it.

### 10. You still need a place to put the output

Nothing here supplies a blog, a channel or a mailing list, and no course does.
Budget one free evening to set those up before day one, or the first week gets
spent on plumbing instead of publishing.

### 11. The one-time price is the real argument

Most AI-content tooling is a monthly subscription that quietly outlives your
interest. A one-time payment does not renew, does not lapse, and does not need
cancelling in six months. If you are the sort of person who has three forgotten

## Common questions

### Do I need to pay for ChatGPT as well?

No. The free tier is enough to follow the system, and most beginners should
start there. A paid AI subscription only becomes worth it once you are
publishing enough that speed and longer outputs actually save you time — which
is a problem you'd love to have in month three.

### Will readers know the content was written with AI?

They will if you publish raw output, which is why the editing pass matters. The
workflow that holds up is: AI drafts, you cut, you add the one thing only you
know. Search engines are fine with AI-assisted content that is genuinely useful;
they are not fine with bulk output nobody needed.

### Is a 60-day guarantee actually honoured?

Digistore24 processes refunds as the platform, not the individual seller, which
is the reason a guarantee like this carries weight. Keep your purchase email,
and request through the platform rather than by chasing the seller. Confirm the
current terms on the checkout page before you buy.

### What if I've already tried and given up once?

Then the useful question is *where* it stopped. If it stopped at "I don't know
what to post", a prompt library and a calendar are aimed exactly at you. If it
stopped at "I never made the time", buy nothing yet — fix the hour first, then
come back.
